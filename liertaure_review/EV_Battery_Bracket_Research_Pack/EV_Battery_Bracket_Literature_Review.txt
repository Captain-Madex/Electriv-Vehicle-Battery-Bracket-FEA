LITERATURE REVIEW AND APPLICATION TO THE EV BATTERY BRACKET FEM PROJECT
============================================================================

The three research papers were reviewed to understand how EV battery brackets
and battery-pack support structures are designed, analysed and optimized using
finite element methods. The literature provides guidance on structural
modelling, material selection, loading conditions, mesh refinement, stress
distribution, deformation and lightweight design.

1. BATTERY BRACKET TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZATION USING 3D PRINTING
-----------------------------------------------------------

Reference:
G. Zhang, X. Li, J. Li, X. Zhou, and Y. Zhou, "Optimization design of battery
bracket for new energy vehicles based on 3D printing technology," Scientific
Reports, vol. 14, Art. no. 13461, 2024, doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-64393-x.

This paper focuses on reducing the weight of an electric vehicle battery
bracket while maintaining adequate structural strength. The authors developed
a 3D model and performed topology optimization. The optimized design was
manufactured using 3D printing and inspected for dimensional accuracy and
assembly compatibility.

Important findings reported by the paper:
- Maximum displacement after optimization: 3.20 mm.
- Maximum von Mises stress: 240.7 MPa.
- Minimum factor of safety: 1.
- Optimized mass: 0.348 kg.
- Reported mass reduction: approximately 49.2%.

What we have taken from this paper:
1. The importance of lightweight design in EV battery support structures.
2. The use of von Mises stress to evaluate structural strength.
3. The importance of monitoring deformation under applied loads.
4. The relationship between material reduction and structural performance.
5. The importance of checking manufacturability and assembly after
   optimization.

We use these concepts to understand the structural behaviour of our battery
bracket and identify areas where material may potentially be reduced without
compromising strength.

2. DESIGN AND ANALYSIS OF BATTERY BRACKET FOR ELECTRIC VEHICLE
---------------------------------------------------------------

Reference:
N. S. Chandrakant and D. S. Galhe, "Design and Analysis of Battery Bracket for
Electric Vehicle," International Journal of Advance Research and Innovative
Ideas in Education, vol. 10, no. 3, 2024.

This paper discusses the design and finite element analysis of an EV battery
bracket. It considers the bracket's role in supporting the battery pack and
maintaining structural integrity under operating loads. The study emphasizes
CAD modelling, material selection, weight optimization and manufacturability.
It also discusses structural simulation approaches.

What we have taken from this paper:
1. The importance of designing a bracket capable of safely supporting the
   battery.
2. The use of CAD geometry as the basis for finite element modelling.
3. The evaluation of stress and deformation under structural loading.
4. The importance of material selection and weight reduction.
5. The need to consider operating conditions when evaluating bracket
   performance.

We use these concepts as a basis for our bracket geometry, structural loading
approach and evaluation of the resulting displacement and stress fields.

3. OPTIMIZATION AND STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS OF AUTOMOTIVE BATTERY PACKS USING ANSYS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reference:
Y. Liu, C. Liu, J. Tan, Y. He, F. Li, and T. Zhang, "Optimization and
Structural Analysis of Automotive Battery Packs Using ANSYS," Symmetry,
vol. 16, no. 11, Art. no. 1464, 2024, doi: 10.3390/sym16111464.

This research investigates the structural performance of an EV battery-pack
assembly using ANSYS. The authors developed a 3D battery-pack model and carried
out static structural and modal analyses.

Three operating conditions were considered:
- Sharp left turn on an uneven road.
- Sharp right turn on an uneven road.
- Emergency braking on an uneven road.

The authors also performed mesh-independence testing using different mesh
sizes. Their final model contained 275,953 elements and 546,089 nodes. The
modal analysis identified the first natural frequency as 33.69 Hz. The study
also investigated structural lightweighting and optimization.

What we have taken from this paper:
1. The importance of applying realistic operating loads to battery-support
   structures.
2. The use of ANSYS for static structural analysis.
3. The importance of mesh refinement and mesh-independence studies.
4. The evaluation of deformation and stress under different loading
   conditions.
5. The importance of structural constraints and proper load transfer.
6. The relevance of modal analysis for future vibration-related investigations.

We use these concepts particularly for our mesh-convergence study, load
application, boundary-condition definition and comparison of numerical FEM
results with ANSYS results.

6. OVERALL CONCLUSION
---------------------

The reviewed literature establishes the importance of structural analysis,
material selection, mesh refinement and optimization in EV battery bracket
design.

The first paper provides guidance on topology optimization and lightweight
design. The second provides direct background on battery bracket design and
finite element analysis. The third provides a broader understanding of
battery-pack structural analysis using ANSYS, including mesh-independence and
modal analysis.

Based on these studies, our project focuses on developing an independent
Python-based FEM solver and validating its results against ANSYS. Particular
attention is given to displacement calculation, stress recovery,
boundary-condition implementation, load distribution and mesh convergence.

The ultimate objective is to establish a reliable computational workflow
that can evaluate the structural behaviour of an EV battery bracket and
provide a foundation for future design optimization.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
---------------------

We have adopted modelling and analysis concepts from these publications. We

results as inputs to our own simulation. Our results must be validated
independently.
