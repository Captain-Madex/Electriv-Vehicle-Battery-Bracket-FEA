ANSYS RESULT SUMMARY vs CUSTOM Q4 FEM SOLVER
EV BATTERY BRACKET - FIVE-MESH COMPARISON
============================================================

1. SCOPE AND SOURCES
--------------------
This comparison pairs the five ANSYS result-summary PDFs with the five
custom Q4 solver JSON summaries by nominal mesh size:

  2.00 mm -> ANSYS PDF "results for 2mm mesh"      vs Mesh 1 JSON
  1.50 mm -> ANSYS PDF "results for 1.5mm mesh"    vs Mesh 2 JSON
  1.00 mm -> ANSYS PDF "results for 1mm mesh"      vs Mesh 3 JSON
  0.50 mm -> ANSYS PDF "results for 0.5mm mesh"    vs Mesh 4 JSON
  0.25 mm -> ANSYS PDF "results for 0.25mm mesh"   vs Mesh 5 JSON

The ANSYS source PDFs are one-page "Solution Quantities and Result Summary"
screenshots dated 11/6/2026. They report minimum/maximum result values.
The custom solver data are the uploaded JSON mesh summaries.

The ANSYS PDFs do not show all model setup information (applied force,
material definitions, exact supports, section thickness, mesh element
counts, coordinate-system definitions, and result averaging settings).
Therefore, the pairing is by filename and mesh size. The comparison is
numerical, but equivalence of all model settings must still be confirmed.

2. KEY DEFINITIONS USED FOR THE ERROR CALCULATION
-------------------------------------------------
For every compared minimum or maximum:

  Signed difference = Q4 solver value - ANSYS value
  Absolute error    = |Q4 solver value - ANSYS value|
  Relative error %  = Absolute error / |ANSYS value| x 100

A signed relative difference is also included in the CSV:
  (Q4 solver value - ANSYS value) / |ANSYS value| x 100

A positive signed difference means the Q4 solver result is numerically higher.
A negative signed difference means it is numerically lower.

For minimum stress values that are negative, the absolute ANSYS reference
value is used in the percentage denominator. This avoids a confusing sign
change in the percentage. Percentage errors are left blank when the ANSYS
reference is effectively zero because percentage error around zero is not
meaningful.

For component peak-magnitude discussion, peak magnitude is defined as:
  max(|minimum value|, |maximum value|)

4. MAIN FINDINGS
----------------

4.1 Total deformation: large, consistent disagreement
-----------------------------------------------------
ANSYS maximum total deformation is approximately 4.86-4.88E-06 mm for all
five meshes. The custom Q4 solver reports approximately 9.64-9.65E-06 mm.

Q4 relative to ANSYS:
  Mesh 1 (2.00 mm): 97.92% higher
  Mesh 2 (1.50 mm): 98.28% higher
  Mesh 3 (1.00 mm): 98.20% higher
  Mesh 4 (0.50 mm): 98.25% higher
  Mesh 5 (0.25 mm): 98.40% higher

The Q4/ANSYS deformation ratio is approximately
1.982 across the five meshes. This is a very consistent
factor close to 1.98, not a random mesh-to-mesh fluctuation.

Interpretation:
  - Both codes show an almost flat maximum-deformation curve with refinement.
  - The absolute displacement level does not agree.
  - A stable factor-of-about-two difference usually points to a systematic
    modelling, scaling, boundary-condition, load, thickness, or postprocessing
    difference rather than ordinary mesh discretization alone.
  - It must not be corrected by simply dividing the Q4 result by two. The
    cause should be identified first.

The directional results also differ. The assumed X-direction peak absolute
displacement is substantially larger in the Q4 result, while the Y-direction
peak absolute displacement is almost twice ANSYS. The PDFs label the fields
"Directional Deformation" and "Directional Deformation 2"; mapping these to
global X and Y is an assumption and should be checked in the ANSYS coordinate
system.

4.2 Maximum von Mises stress: relatively close, but mesh trend differs
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
ANSYS maximum equivalent stress:
  0.15211, 0.15933, 0.17296, 0.21403, 0.26134 MPa

Q4 maximum von Mises stress:
  0.12126, 0.14602, 0.15198, 0.20480, 0.27908 MPa

Q4 relative to ANSYS:
  Mesh 1 (2.00 mm): -20.28% (Q4 lower)
  Mesh 2 (1.50 mm): -8.35% (Q4 lower)
  Mesh 3 (1.00 mm): -12.13% (Q4 lower)
  Mesh 4 (0.50 mm): -4.31% (Q4 lower)
  Mesh 5 (0.25 mm): +6.79% (Q4 higher)

The closest maximum von Mises agreement is at Mesh 4 (0.50 mm), with an
absolute relative difference of 4.31%. At Mesh 5 the Q4 result
is +6.79% higher than ANSYS. The difference is not monotonic:
Q4 is below ANSYS for the first four meshes, then above it at the finest.

Both methods show peak von Mises stress rising with refinement. This is
consistent with local stress concentrations becoming more resolved. However,
the peak-stress values are not yet mesh-independent in either dataset.

4.3 Equivalent elastic strain: disagreement grows at fine meshes
----------------------------------------------------------------
Q4 maximum equivalent elastic strain relative to ANSYS:
  Mesh 1 (2.00 mm): +9.86% higher
  Mesh 2 (1.50 mm): +4.89% higher
  Mesh 3 (1.00 mm): +9.56% higher
  Mesh 4 (0.50 mm): +27.15% higher
  Mesh 5 (0.25 mm): +49.76% higher

The Q4 equivalent strain is higher at every mesh. The difference increases
substantially at the two finest meshes, reaching 49.76% at
0.25 mm. This is a significant mismatch and should be investigated alongside
the stress and displacement postprocessing.

Observations:
  - sigma_y peak magnitude is lower in Q4 at all meshes, although the gap
    narrows with refinement.
  - shear stress magnitude is lower in Q4 at all meshes; the gap also
    narrows with refinement.
  - sigma_x behaves differently: Q4 is lower in the coarser meshes, then

    but the difference narrows toward the finest mesh.
  - The maximum-principal tensile peak is far lower in Q4 than ANSYS in all
    five meshes. This comparison is especially sensitive to whether the two
    postprocessors are reporting the same principal-stress field and sampling
    convention; verify this before treating it as a direct physical error.

6. IMPORTANT COMPARABILITY LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------
A. The PDFs are result summaries only. They do not prove the two models used
   identical applied force, support locations, material assignments,
   thickness/section settings, or local coordinate systems.

B. The ANSYS labels "Normal Stress", "Normal Stress 2", "Directional
   Deformation", and "Directional Deformation 2" do not show explicit global
   axis names in the screenshots. The CSV maps them to sigma_x, sigma_y, Ux,
   and Uy respectively as a working assumption. Confirm this in ANSYS.

C. Maximum/minimum values may be sampled differently:
   - The custom Q4 summary states that stresses/strains are based on
     element-averaged 2x2 Gauss-point values, with von Mises taken as the
     maximum across the four Gauss points.
   - Its displacement values are nodal values.
   - The ANSYS result-summary screenshot does not show whether values are
     nodal, elemental, extrapolated, or averaged.
   Different sampling and averaging can change local extrema.

D. The custom Q4 model summary describes a 2-D plane-stress membrane
   approximation from SHELL181 in-plane connectivity. It does not include
   shell rotations, bending, transverse shear, or through-thickness stress.
   Confirm the ANSYS output is extracted from the same physical idealization
   before interpreting all differences as solver error.

E. The Q4 JSON summaries report section thickness 80 mm for all three
   sections. Confirm that this is intentional and exactly matches the ANSYS
   section definition. A thickness mismatch directly changes stiffness and
   stress/displacement response.

7. MOST LIKELY CHECKS TO PERFORM
-------------------------------
Priority 1 - Verify identical model inputs:
  - Same total applied load and load direction
  - Same loaded-edge length and load distribution
  - Same constrained geometry and support DOFs
  - Same material assigned to each region
  - Same Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio
  - Same section thickness for each material region
  - Same geometry and interface coordinates

Priority 2 - Resolve the deformation factor:
  - Export ANSYS nodal Ux and Uy at selected identical node coordinates.
  - Export Q4 nodal Ux and Uy at those same physical coordinates.
  - Compare each component at the same point, not only global maxima.
  - Recalculate total deformation consistently as sqrt(Ux^2 + Uy^2).
  - Check mm versus m conversion and any scale factor in the JSON writer.
  - Check load normalization and whether total load is applied once or twice.
  - Check that section thickness is not applied twice or omitted in the
    stiffness/load formulation.

Priority 3 - Resolve local stress differences:
  - Record maximum von Mises stress coordinates and element IDs in both
    solvers for every mesh.
  - Compare stress at the same physical coordinates and on the same material
    side of each bonded interface.
  - Compare integration-point/Gauss-point stress separately from
    extrapolated and nodally averaged stress.
  - Inspect the support edge, loaded edge, corners, and bonded interfaces.
  - Review the 168 degenerate elements excluded from the 0.25 mm Q4 mesh and
    check whether excluded elements occur near critical regions.

Priority 4 - Compare tensor and principal definitions:
  - Confirm the ANSYS coordinate system for Normal Stress and Normal Stress 2.
  - Confirm whether ANSYS "Maximum Principal Stress" is the maximum principal
    eigenvalue and whether the Q4 field uses the same sign and sampling rule.
  - Compute both principal stresses from the same sigma_x, sigma_y, tau_xy
    tensor at matched integration points.

8. INTERPRETATION OF THE ERROR
------------------------------
The reported differences are not all the same type:

  - Total deformation: systematic Q4 overprediction by about 98% at every
    mesh. This is too consistent to attribute to mesh refinement alone.
  - Von Mises stress: relatively close at 0.5 mm, but differences change
    sign and the finest mesh has Q4 above ANSYS.
  - Equivalent strain: Q4 above ANSYS at every mesh, with a growing gap at
    fine resolution.
  - Stress components: mixed agreement; some components are systematically
    below ANSYS while sigma_x becomes much higher on fine meshes.
  - Principal stress: minimum-principal compression is moderately lower in
    Q4, while maximum-principal tensile peaks show a large mismatch.

Consequently, the Q4 solver should not yet be declared validated against
ANSYS. The comparison demonstrates partial agreement in the von Mises
magnitude and a strong mesh-stable trend in each solver's total deformation,
but it also reveals systematic deformation disagreement and significant
local-field differences.

9. FINAL CONCLUSION
-------------------
Across all five mesh sizes, the custom Q4 solver and ANSYS show similar
qualitative trends in several quantities, particularly increasing peak
equivalent stress with mesh refinement and nearly unchanged maximum total
deformation within each solver's own mesh series.

However, the absolute results do not agree consistently. The custom Q4
solver predicts almost twice the ANSYS maximum total deformation at every
mesh. Its peak von Mises stress is within about 4.3% at 0.5 mm but differs
by about 6.8% in the opposite direction at 0.25 mm. Equivalent elastic
strain is higher in Q4 throughout and differs by about 49.8% at the finest
mesh. Normal and shear stress components show mixed, mesh-dependent
differences, while principal-stress maxima require a careful definition and
sampling check.

The next validation step should be a point-to-point comparison of nodal
displacements and integration-point stresses at identical physical
coordinates, after confirming identical loads, constraints, materials,
section thickness, and coordinate-system definitions. Only after these
checks should the remaining difference be attributed to the Q4 formulation
or solver implementation.
