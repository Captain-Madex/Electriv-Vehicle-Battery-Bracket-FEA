EV BATTERY BRACKET FEM
MESH CONVERGENCE AND RESULTS-PATTERN EXPLANATION
================================================

Purpose
-------
This document explains the five uploaded mesh-result summaries and the
patterns observed as the nominal element size is reduced from 2.0 mm to
0.25 mm. It is based on the values in the five JSON result summaries.

The five cases are:
  Mesh 1: 2.00 mm
  Mesh 2: 1.50 mm
  Mesh 3: 1.00 mm
  Mesh 4: 0.50 mm
  Mesh 5: 0.25 mm

IMPORTANT MODEL DESCRIPTION AND LIMITATION
------------------------------------------
All five source summaries describe the model as:

  "2-D Q4 plane-stress membrane approximation using SHELL181 in-plane
   connectivity."

The model represents in-plane Ux and Uy membrane response. According to the
source summaries, it does NOT solve shell rotations, bending, transverse
shear, or through-thickness stress. It is therefore not a complete SHELL181
shell analysis and should not be described as a full 3-D bracket solution.

This distinction matters for a battery bracket because out-of-plane bending,
local bending around connections, and through-thickness effects may be
important in the real component. The present convergence conclusions apply
to the reported 2-D membrane model only.

The five summaries also report a section thickness of 80 mm for each of the
three material sections in every mesh. This value is repeated consistently
in the summaries, but it should be checked against the intended physical
thickness and ANSYS section definition. Do not confuse this section-thickness
value with the nominal mesh size.

2. MESH SIZE AND DISCRETIZATION
-------------------------------

  Mesh       Nominal size     Nodes       Valid Q4       Degenerate
  1          2.00 mm          1,498       1,288          6
  2          1.50 mm          2,513       2,233          10
  3          1.00 mm          5,471       5,054          10
  4          0.50 mm         20,761      19,922          40
  5          0.25 mm         81,984      80,265         168

The number of valid Q4 elements grows from 1,288 to 80,265. The final mesh
contains about 62.3 times as many valid Q4 elements as Mesh 1. The number of
nodes grows from 1,498 to 81,984.

This large increase in element count is normal for refinement. In a
two-dimensional region, halving a broadly uniform element size often creates
roughly four times as many elements. The Mesh 4 to Mesh 5 element-count ratio
is about 4.03, which is close to that expectation. The earlier ratios differ
because the nominal sizes, geometry partitioning, local sizing, and mesh
topology need not form an exact uniform refinement sequence.

Degenerate elements were excluded from the Q4 solution in all cases:
  Mesh 1: 6
  Mesh 2: 10
  Mesh 3: 10
  Mesh 4: 40
  Mesh 5: 168

The excluded count increases as the mesh becomes finer. This does not by
itself prove that the solution is invalid, but it makes mesh-quality review
important. Confirm why these elements are degenerate, where they occur, and
whether their exclusion affects load paths, interfaces, or stress hot spots.
A refinement study should use a consistent and justified element-quality
filter for every mesh.

3.1 Total deformation: nearly stable
------------------------------------

The maximum total deformation changes only slightly as the mesh is refined:

  Mesh 1: 9.65416697E-06 mm
  Mesh 2: 9.65290882E-06 mm
  Mesh 3: 9.64334442E-06 mm
  Mesh 4: 9.63929598E-06 mm
  Mesh 5: 9.64047952E-06 mm

From Mesh 1 to Mesh 5, the maximum deformation decreases by approximately
0.1418%. Between Mesh 4 and Mesh 5, it increases by approximately 0.0123%.
The last two values are therefore extremely close.

The sequence is not strictly decreasing: the Mesh 5 value is slightly higher
than Mesh 4. This small change is consistent with a stable plateau rather
than a meaningful increase in global deformation.

Interpretation:
  - The global in-plane deformation response is nearly mesh-independent over
    the tested range.
  - The overall stiffness/compliance predicted by these five cases appears
    stable with respect to this displacement measure.
  - The conclusion applies to maximum total deformation in this model; it
    does not prove that every local displacement or every stress is converged.

4. STRESS CONVERGENCE AND STRESS COMPONENT PATTERNS
--------------------------------------------------

4.1 Maximum von Mises stress: not converged
-------------------------------------------

  Mesh       Maximum von Mises stress (MPa)    Change from previous mesh
  1          0.12125563                        -
  2          0.14602425                        +20.43%
  3          0.15198395                        +4.08%
  4          0.20479828                        +34.75%
  5          0.27908044                        +36.27%

From Mesh 1 to Mesh 5, the reported maximum von Mises stress increases by
approximately 130.16%. The Mesh 5 maximum is about 2.30 times the Mesh 1
maximum. Between Mesh 4 and Mesh 5 alone, it increases by approximately
36.27%.

There is no plateau in the reported maximum stress. Therefore, the maximum
von Mises stress is NOT converged over the tested mesh range.

A rising peak stress during refinement can have several explanations:
  - A finer mesh is resolving a steep stress gradient that a coarse mesh
    smooths out.
  - The maximum may be near a sharp re-entrant corner or geometric feature.
  - The maximum may be near a support, load boundary, or bonded interface.
  - A local idealization may create a stress singularity or near-singular
    concentration.
  - The location of the maximum may move between meshes.
  - Element shape, local distortion, or treatment of excluded degenerate
    elements may affect the reported maximum.

These are possible explanations, not confirmed causes. The summaries alone
do not identify the coordinates or physical location of the maximum. The
maximum-stress location must be extracted for each mesh before selecting the
correct explanation.

4.6 Overall stress pattern
--------------------------

The X-normal, Y-normal, shear, and principal-stress ranges all become wider
as the mesh is refined. This is consistent with the rising maximum von Mises
stress: the local stress field is still sensitive to mesh resolution.

A peak value is especially sensitive to a single local element or integration
point. It can change even when most of the structure's stress field is
stable. The current summaries provide minimum and maximum values, not
coordinate-matched stress histories or stress-path values. They cannot show
whether the same physical point is responsible for the maximum in every mesh.

The rising peak equivalent strain is consistent with the rising peak stress.
However, the summaries do not prove that the maximum strain and maximum
stress occur at exactly the same location.

8. OVERALL CONVERGENCE JUDGEMENT
-------------------------------

  Quantity                         Assessment
  Maximum total deformation        Nearly converged / stable
  Maximum absolute X displacement  Stable trend
  Maximum-magnitude Y displacement Stable trend
  Maximum von Mises stress         Not converged
  Normal stress X extrema          Not converged / mesh-sensitive
  Normal stress Y extrema          Not converged / mesh-sensitive
  Shear stress XY extrema          Not converged / mesh-sensitive
  Principal stress extrema        Not converged / mesh-sensitive
  Maximum equivalent strain        Not converged
  Global force equilibrium         Satisfied in all five cases
  Bonded interface counts          Increase consistently with refinement

The most important pattern is the difference between global and local
quantities:

  GLOBAL RESPONSE:
    Maximum deformation changes by only about 0.142% from Mesh 1 to Mesh 5.
    This indicates that the global in-plane displacement response is stable.

  LOCAL RESPONSE:
    Maximum von Mises stress rises by about 130.16%.
    Maximum equivalent strain rises by about 134.21%.
    Normal, shear, and principal stress extrema also increase in magnitude.
    These local quantities are not yet mesh-independent.

It is possible for displacement to converge before peak stress. Displacement
is a global response, whereas a maximum stress is a local extreme that can
be strongly affected by a small region, a geometric corner, an interface, or
a boundary-condition idealization.

9. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS
-------------------------

Do not immediately create another globally finer mesh without first finding
where the maximum stress occurs. Follow this sequence:

Step 1 - Locate each peak:
  Export the coordinates, element ID, material ID, and stress value of the
  maximum von Mises stress for each mesh. Also record the coordinates and
  values of the maximum equivalent strain and important component-stress
  extrema.

Step 2 - Check whether the peak moves:
  Plot or list the peak coordinates for Mesh 1 through Mesh 5. Determine
  whether the maximum stays at the same physical feature or jumps between
  locations.

Step 3 - Inspect the local geometry:
  Review sharp internal corners, re-entrant corners, material interfaces,
  tied boundaries, load application edges, and fixed support edges. A peak
  at an idealized sharp corner or boundary may behave like a stress
  singularity.

Step 4 - Check mesh quality:
  Inspect element aspect ratio, skewness, Jacobian quality, element
  orientation, and the locations of all excluded degenerate elements.
  Confirm that element filtering is consistent across the five meshes.

Step 5 - Compare a fixed physical location:
  Extract stress at the same physical coordinate or along the same physical
  path for every mesh. For a material interface, consistently sample the
  same material side. Do not compare only each mesh's unconstrained global
  maximum if the peak location changes.

Step 6 - Compare a finite region:
  In addition to the absolute maximum, compare an average or percentile
  stress over the same small physical region. A fixed-region average or a
  high percentile can be more informative when a single-point peak is
  singular or unusually sensitive.

Step 7 - Refine locally:
  Once the peak location and cause are understood, refine that region while
  keeping the geometry, loads, supports, materials, section definitions,
  and extraction procedure consistent.

Step 8 - Reassess the model dimensionality:
  If bracket bending or out-of-plane effects are important, build and verify
  a suitable shell or 3-D solid model. The present 2-D membrane model cannot
  establish convergence of bending or through-thickness stresses.

10. WHY A FORMAL GCI VALUE IS NOT GIVEN
---------------------------------------

A formal Grid Convergence Index (GCI) or Richardson extrapolation value is
not reported in this document.

The nominal mesh sizes are 2.0, 1.5, 1.0, 0.5, and 0.25 mm. Their successive
refinement ratios are not all identical. In addition, the maximum stress
does not show a plateau or an established asymptotic convergent trend.
The meshes also contain different numbers of excluded degenerate elements.

A formal GCI study requires a justified systematic refinement family,
consistent geometry and result extraction, and evidence that the selected
quantity is in an appropriate convergence regime. The present data are
sufficient to identify trends and assess displacement stability, but they
are not sufficient to claim a reliable stress GCI.

11. REPORT-READY CONCLUSION
---------------------------

A mesh refinement study was performed for the EV battery bracket using five
nominal mesh sizes: 2.0 mm, 1.5 mm, 1.0 mm, 0.5 mm, and 0.25 mm. The number
of valid Q4 elements increased from 1,288 in Mesh 1 to 80,265 in Mesh 5.
The applied load, material definitions, geometry bounds, and named support
conditions were kept consistent in the uploaded result summaries. The total
applied load was 294.3 N in the negative Y direction.

The maximum total deformation remained nearly unchanged during refinement,
decreasing from 9.65417E-06 mm to 9.64048E-06 mm. The overall change was
approximately 0.142%, and the difference between the final two meshes was
approximately 0.0123%. The global in-plane displacement response can
therefore be considered nearly converged over the tested mesh range.

The local stress and strain results showed a different pattern. The maximum
von Mises stress increased from 0.12126 MPa to 0.27908 MPa, an increase of
approximately 130.16%. The maximum equivalent elastic strain increased from
8.35506E-07 to 1.95687E-06 mm/mm, an increase of approximately 134.21%.
The normal, shear, and principal stress extrema also increased in magnitude
as the mesh was refined. These results indicate that the local peak stress
and strain fields are still mesh-sensitive and have not demonstrated
convergence.

The reaction forces balanced the applied 294.3 N load in all five cases,
confirming global force equilibrium. Nevertheless, equilibrium does not
establish local stress convergence. The next stage should identify the
coordinates and element locations of the peak stress in every mesh, inspect
the associated geometry and boundary conditions, and compare stress at
consistent physical locations or over a fixed region.

Finally, all conclusions apply only to the 2-D Q4 plane-stress membrane
approximation represented in the supplied summaries. Since shell rotations,
bending, transverse shear, and through-thickness stresses are not included,
a full shell or 3-D solid analysis may be needed to assess the complete
structural response of the EV battery bracket.
