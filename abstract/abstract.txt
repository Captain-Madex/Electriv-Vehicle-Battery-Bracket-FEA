ABSTRACT
=================================================================
Static structural analysis of an end plate + L-bracket (with gusset)
+ clamp plate supporting a battery block: Python Q4 FEM solver,
mesh convergence and ANSYS comparison
=================================================================


This study analyses the static structural response of an end plate,
L-bracket with gusset, and clamp plate that support a 60 kg battery
block. By symmetry, each bracket carries half the block weight, 294.3 N.
The assembly is modelled as a 2D plane-stress section (80 mm thick) with
bonded interfaces. The end-plate face and flange bottom are fixed, and
the load is applied as a uniform pressure on the clamp plate. An
independent Python solver using four-node quadrilateral (Q4) elements
was developed to read the ANSYS CDB mesh, solve for nodal displacements,
and recover element stresses and strains. The results were compared
with ANSYS across five mesh sizes: 2.00, 1.50, 1.00, 0.50 and 0.25 mm.

The mesh-convergence study gives two different outcomes. Maximum total
deformation is effectively mesh-independent in both solvers. ANSYS
changes by about 0.4% (4.88E-06 to 4.86E-06 mm) and the Q4 solver by
about 0.1% (about 9.65E-06 to 9.64E-06 mm). Peak von Mises stress has
not converged. It rises with refinement from 0.152 to 0.261 MPa in ANSYS
and from 0.121 to 0.279 MPa in the Q4 solver, consistent with local
stress concentrations being resolved progressively.

Agreement between the two solvers is partial. The Q4 solver overpredicts
maximum deformation by a near-constant factor of about 1.98 at every
mesh. Peak von Mises stress differs from ANSYS by -20.3% to +6.8%, and
the best agreement (-4.3%) is at 0.50 mm. The Q4 solver therefore
cannot yet be considered validated. The next step is to confirm
identical inputs (load, supports, thickness, materials) and compare
displacements and stresses at matching coordinates.


Keywords: finite element analysis, battery bracket, Q4 plane-stress
element, mesh convergence, ANSYS validation, von Mises stress
